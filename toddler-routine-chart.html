Toddler routine charts by age (2–6): morning, bedtime and leaving the house

By Hau Ha Cong, maker of the Tiny Steps app · Updated 17 November 2026

Short answer: a toddler routine works best as 3–8 short steps, always in the same order, each shown as a picture. Two-year-olds manage 3–4 steps with help; by age 5–6 most children can follow 6–8 steps on their own. Keep the first step easy, say each step out loud, and finish with a small celebration.

How many steps for each age?

AgeSteps per routineWhat helps most
2 years3–4One picture at a time, an adult doing the step with them, lots of praise
3 years4–5Pictures plus a short spoken cue ("Shoes on!"), a timer for slow steps
4 years5–6Letting them tick steps off themselves, a star or sticker at the end
5–6 years6–8Choosing the order of one or two steps, weekday and weekend versions

These are starting points from everyday family use, not rules. If a routine regularly falls apart, remove a step before adding pressure.

How to make a visual schedule your toddler follows

  1. Use pictures, not words. Children under 6 mostly can't read yet; one clear picture per step is enough.
  2. Show one step at a time. A whole chart at once can overwhelm a 2- or 3-year-old.
  3. Say the step out loud. Hearing "Brush teeth!" together with the picture makes it stick.
  4. Add a gentle timer to slow steps like breakfast or getting dressed, so "how long" is visible.
  5. Celebrate the finish, not perfection. A star, a high five or a happy dance at the end.
  6. Keep the order the same on school days; make a separate, shorter weekend version.

Questions parents ask

At what age can a toddler follow a routine chart?

Many 2-year-olds can follow a 3–4 step picture routine with an adult beside them. Following it more independently usually comes between ages 3 and 5.

Paper chart or app?

A printed chart is simple and free. An app can show one step at a time, read steps aloud, run a timer and keep the routine the same on every family member's phone.

What if my toddler refuses a step?

Offer a small choice inside the step ("blue socks or red socks?"), shorten the routine, and keep the celebration at the end. Avoid taking stars away.
